Disney Pocahontas

What's the truth behind Disney Pocahontas???

Disney’s Pocahontas is very different from the true story of Pocahontas. The real Pocahontas was a Powhatan girl who was born around 1596. She was also known by the names Amonute and Matoaka, while Pocahontas was most likely a nickname. She was the daughter of Wahunsenacawh, a powerful Powhatan leader. In the Disney movie, Pocahontas is shown as a young woman who falls in love with John Smith. In reality, she was only about 10 to 12 years old when she met John Smith, who was around 27 years old. There is no historical evidence that the two were ever romantically involved. The famous story of Pocahontas saving John Smith’s life is also debated because historians are unsure whether the event happened the way Smith described it.

The real Pocahontas also experienced a much more difficult life than the one shown in the Disney movie. In 1613, English colonists captured Pocahontas and held her hostage. During her captivity, she converted to Christianity and was given the name Rebecca. She later married an English tobacco planter named John Rolfe in 1614, rather than John Smith. Pocahontas and John Rolfe had a son named Thomas together. She eventually traveled with her family to England, where she was introduced to English society. While preparing to return to Virginia, Pocahontas became seriously ill and died in 1617 when she was only about 20 or 21 years old. Her true story shows that Disney changed many important parts of her life to create a more romantic and family-friendly movie.

Disney's Pocahontas vs. The Real Pocahontas

Topic Disney's Pocahontas The Real Pocahontas
Relationship with John Smith Pocahontas and John Smith fall in love and have a close romantic relationship. Pocahontas was about 10-12 years old when she met John Smith (who was 27). There is no evidence of a romantic relationship.
Saving John Smith Pocahontas throws herself over John Smith to stop her father from killing him. This event is disputed. John Smith wrote about it, but historians believe it may have been a misunderstanding of a Powhatan ceremony.
Pocahontas's life and choices She chooses to stay in her village and follow her heart. She was captured by English colonists in 1613 and held hostage. She did not have the freedom to choose.
Marriage Pocahontas does not get married in the movie. She married English tobacco planter John Rolfe in 1614, not John Smith.
Later life and legacy The movie ends with peace between the English and Powhatan people and a happy, hopeful future. Pocahontas traveled to England, was presented to English society, had a son named Thomas, and died in 1617 at about 20-21 years old.
